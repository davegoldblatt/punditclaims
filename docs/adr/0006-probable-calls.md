# 0006 Probable calls: the error bar on the answer

Date: 2026-10-29. Owner decision. Policy 2026-10-28.7 is unchanged.

The strict review settles a claim only when two providers agree that the evidence meets criteria frozen before grading. On release 20261029T225230Z that left 1,882 published claims unresolved and 3,594 held. The owner: "having like 1000 unresolved is really unsatisfying, when a reasonable person would prob call them resolved". He asked for an "error bar" rather than a lower bar.

1. **The question.** Both providers answer one looser question per claim: on the evidence in the frozen packet, would a reasonable, informed reader call this claim today? The answer is `probably_right`, `probably_wrong`, or `too_early`, with a short reason (prompt `research/prompts/probable-1.md`). They judge only the packet: no new search and no remembered facts. Forecasts before their deadline, hedged words, opinions, and thin or mixed evidence are `too_early`.
2. **Which claims.** A published claim whose current outcome is unresolved, and a held claim that was graded against evidence, unless it is held for a doubt about its words or reading (attribution, quotation, source, or a reviewer reading the claim differently). A failed final-text check is about our wording, so those claims qualify. Screened-out claims have no evidence and do not qualify.
3. **The rule.** Each answer is a public review run (`batch_probable`) bound to the case digest, and both providers must have answered (`src/lib/probable.ts`). The same call from both is a **reasonable** call. A call from one, with `too_early` from the other, is a **speculative** call. Opposite calls are no call. A new contract, packet, or claim revision unbinds the answers.
4. **What it changes.** Nothing in any assessment. The release carries the call and its level on the claim or held record. The home page and person pages have a "How sure?" switch (owner request, 2026-10-29): strict (the default, the answer from settled claims), reasonable, and speculative. Each level shows its own answer in words, names itself beside it, draws its calls as lighter squares, and gives its counts. The switch is CSS only (`:has()`) on the hero, and hides itself where `:has()` is missing, so the page stays strict. `check-dist` verifies the probable counts at every level. The totals, shares, and answer words at each level are computed from those counts on the page and are not separately verified. The method page explains it at `#probable`.
5. **Cost.** About 4,100 claims, six to a call, both providers, medium effort. A pilot of 46 claims gave a call on 8.

**Amended 2026-10-30, after the cross-vendor review (gpt-6-astra, xhigh).** Fixed: a probable call now attaches only to the case as it stands (latest claim revision, contract, and packet; never a stale binding), so a regrade with fairer criteria cannot inherit the old call. A held claim is ineligible when any bound strict review doubts its attribution or its reading, not only when the gate's first failure says so. A provider counts as having answered only when its run holds exactly one answer for the claim. Above strict, the level is named beside each answer, the strict answer stays visible, and each level says what it still leaves out. On Explore, held entries sort by date, link to their place in the held list, offer no quote or evidence actions, and are counted apart from the published claims; the colour key stays when nothing matches.

Still open: the release keeps only the call and level, not the two providers' reasons, so a reader cannot yet see why a claim is "probably right". The next step is to carry both reasons into the release and show them on the claim page and the held list.
